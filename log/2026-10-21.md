### Learned

- **W12's first question: yes, the proof can be put on BLS12-381**, by `tfhe-zk-pok` as shipped. Not
  through tfhe-rs's own API, which fixes BLS12-446 and keeps the encryption's randomness private.
- **X5's claim fails as written.** A request's `VERIFY` frame cannot check the input's proof itself:
  the floor is 7.9 times the testnet's 500,000 and 39 times EIP-8141's 100,000. The two precompile
  calls alone are 3.6 times the testnet's budget.
- The obstacle is the verifier's design, not the curve: a transcript over the whole key and
  ciphertext, a matrix squeezed from it byte by byte, and a G2 MSM with challenge scalars.

### For the owner

- **X5, the input side, needs a decision.** Three ways on, none of them this session's to choose:
  - (a) record X5's claim as failing on the input side, and keep the result's side, the committee's
    threshold signature checked in `VERIFY`, which experiment 41 does not touch;
  - (b) prove the same statement with another proof whose verifier the EVM prices well, a SNARK
    over the encryption relation: that is no longer the pinned, unmodified `tfhe-zk-pok`, so it
    departs from the phase's rule for third-party code;
  - (c) let the request's input proof be checked off chain by the committee, which is the verifier
    signing that the claim set out to remove.
- **No fork of tfhe-rs is asked for.** A tfhe-rs ciphertext would need one of two small changes to
  be proved on BLS12-381 (make its `zk` module generic over the curve, or expose the encryption's
  random vectors), but neither changes the floor.
