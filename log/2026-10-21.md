`experiment/gradient`, from `experiment/frames`.

### Done

- **Experiment 50** (branch `exp/50-device-key`): the claim, what would show it wrong, done when
  and the plan pushed before any code or run in `2f71ff0`; the kernel, the registry version 3
  and its control, the run, seven fixtures, the offline rebuild and the results in `7e02d63`.
  - **Simulated:** the owner key is a software P-256 key. No enclave is tested.
  - EIP-7951's `P256VERIFY` answers at `0x100` on the testnet (ethrex `64b8fc09`).
  - Three updates of notes owned by P-256 keys mined, blocks 491,109 to 491,117: two with the
    signature checked by the precompile in `VERIFY` (A `0x30978e72…`, C `0x545b0204…`), one by
    the protocol's P256 scheme (B `0x4a2a10d3…`). The control, the same update with the check
    off, `0x5d83710e…`, block 491,120.
  - The owner check adds 7,372 gas to `VERIFY` (C against the control), 9,504 to the
    transaction. The whole `VERIFY` is 243,355, over EIP-8141's 100,000 because of the Groth16
    check, as in experiment 32.
  - Refused in simulation and at `eth_sendRawTransaction`: a stranger's key, high `s`, no
    signature with valid secrets and proof, and the owner's signature moved onto an update with
    another recipient. The prover refuses a proof under another owner.
- **kohaku-rs `0f04ed1`** (branch `experiment/gradient`): the crate `kohaku-frame-device-key`
  rebuilds experiment 50's updates byte for byte, verifies their proofs and owner signatures,
  and reproduces the three signature refusals offline (4 of 4). Clippy with `-D warnings` clean.
- Suite on `exp/50-device-key`: 1,214 of 1,214; `tsc` clean; `scripts/check_private_names.py`
  clean.

### Learned

- **Done when: met. Would show it wrong: not observed.** The check fits the 100,000 budget with
  more than 90,000 to spare; the precompile is present and accepts a valid signature; the key
  signs a bare 32-byte digest; nobody else takes part; the secrets alone no longer spend.
- The registry must enforce low `s` itself: the precompile accepts both, and an ARBITRARY entry
  is outside the signature hash.
- The owner key is public in each update, so one key across many notes links them. The change
  note named a fresh key.
- A key made inside an enclave cannot come from experiment 45's seed. A note it owns is lost with
  the device unless the wallet arranges another way to spend it. Not built here.

## The gradient session: G4, a remote enclave simulated, attestation against proof

### Done

- **Experiment 51** (branch `exp/51-remote-enclave`, with `exp/50-device-key` merged in so that
  this log stays one file): the claim, what would show it wrong, done when and the plan pushed
  before any code or run in `65d91f1`; the enclave process, the circuit and its key, the
  outcome contract, the run, four fixtures, the offline rebuild and the results in `cdd7f64`.
  - **Simulated:** the enclave is a process on this machine with its Baby Jubjub key sealed
    under its own source's SHA-256; the device secret and the attestation key are the host's.
  - Alice's and Bob's bids, encrypted to the enclave, posted on experiment 33's board
    (`0x72e0a5ca…`, block 491,263). The enclave's one output was mined twice: on its
    attestation, `P256VERIFY` in `VERIFY` (A `0xe294edcc…`, 491,265, `VERIFY` 9,696), and on its
    Groth16 proof (B `0xd6a2834c…`, 491,266, `VERIFY` 245,699).
  - The attestation key signing a wrong output, or for a measurement that never ran, was
    accepted in simulation, as the plan predicted. The proof with the output flipped, another
    ciphertext or another enclave key was refused. A changed enclave source could not open the
    seal.
- **kohaku-rs `8c087ae`** (branch `experiment/gradient`): the crate `kohaku-frame-enclave`
  lands the outcome contract at its address from the deployed initcode, rebuilds both outputs
  byte for byte and checks both routes' evidence (3 of 3); `kohaku-frame-device-key` now shares
  its snarkjs key parser. Clippy with `-D warnings` clean on both.
- Suite on `exp/51-remote-enclave` with 50 merged: 1,236 of 1,236; `tsc` clean;
  `scripts/check_private_names.py` clean.

### Learned

- **Done when: met. Would show it wrong: not observed.** Both routes fit the testnet's
  500,000; nothing checkable shows the bids unread, since the proof needs the decryption key.
- Only the attestation fits EIP-8141's 100,000. The proof costs 25 times more in `VERIFY`.
- The proof takes the attestation key out of integrity and leaves the enclave and its operator
  in privacy exactly where they were.
- A wrong attestation beside a proof would be public evidence of a fault: two outputs for one
  statement, one signed, one proved. Derived from the simulation, not mined.
- Communication: one round, 96 bytes per owner, no interaction between owners; the enclave's run
  took 3,683 ms here.
